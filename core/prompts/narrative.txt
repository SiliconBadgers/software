The lighthouse keeper had not spoken to another person in eleven days when the small red boat appeared on the horizon. She watched it through the salt-streaked window, counting the seconds between each swell, and decided it was too small to be the supply ship and too steady to be a fisherman lost in the fog. When it finally reached the rocks below, a boy of perhaps twelve stepped onto the landing with a wet canvas bag over his shoulder. He did not knock. He climbed the two hundred and six steps without stopping, set the bag on her table, and said that his grandfather had sent him with a letter and a jar of plum preserves. She opened the letter slowly. Inside was a single drawing of the lighthouse as it had looked forty years earlier, before the new lamp was installed, with a tiny figure waving from the gallery. She recognized the handwriting at once, and for the first time that winter she laughed out loud.
